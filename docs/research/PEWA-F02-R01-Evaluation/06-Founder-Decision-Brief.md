# PEWA-F02-R01-B — Founder Decision Brief

**Purpose:** Bound the decisions that should be made before a later taxonomy reconciliation draft. The questions below do not ask the Founder to approve a taxonomy in this review.

## Decisions needed to direct later reconciliation

### 1. Regional scope and evidence threshold

**Question:** Should the next reconciliation compare all African regions covered by any consultant, or treat Great Lakes/East Africa as the first review lens while keeping other regions in the comparison?

**Evidence:** C3 covers multiple regions but explicitly marks Burundi, local language and several cuisines weak (D1 §§4.B, 6); C4 proposes broad region families but relies on secondary material (D1 §2; D5 §5); C6 details East African gaps and lists regions not researched (D5 §§3–4); C5 claims broad fieldwork without an auditable corpus.

**Tradeoff:** A broad comparison preserves continental coverage but may imply evidence parity that does not exist. A Great Lakes-first lens improves local relevance but cannot be treated as a continent-wide standard. No choice requires new research now.

### 2. Treatment of staples, roles and meal composition

**Question:** In the later comparison, should the team first test whether staples are culinary categories, meal roles, separately sellable items, or a combination of these, before comparing proposed parent-child hierarchies?

**Evidence:** C1 puts East African staples under Mains (C003); C2 combines Matooke/Cassava as Tubers (CAT-001-A); C3 has Staples & Starch Accompaniments plus a co-primary “Staple” role; C4 CAT-020 gives Staples/Swallow a top-level category; C5 uses Traditional Starches & Swallows; C6 has Starches & Staples but a parent mismatch for FAM-R01.

**Tradeoff:** Testing multiple object roles avoids choosing a taxonomy level prematurely but may defer a simple display decision. Matooke placement remains ambiguous in the submitted evidence and should not be adjudicated from this scorecard alone.

### 3. Allergen and dietary-claim review gate

**Question:** Before any later draft labels an allergen or dietary field as a candidate, should Founder require a separate evidence review for allergen identity, unknown/declared/cross-contact state, recipe provenance and jurisdictional scope?

**Evidence:** C1 combines peanuts and tree nuts in T002; C5’s nine allergen rows do not match its “14 major allergens” statement, and its Isombe example says Nut-Free despite D1 describing peanut paste; C6 separates peanut and tree-nut rows but also proposes `Nut-free` as a combined claim; C3’s D3 is missing. Assessment issues ISS-025 and ISS-027 record the related findings.

**Tradeoff:** A separate review gate slows any future publication decision but keeps severe safety claims visible outside aggregate scores. This evaluation does not prescribe the allergen set or legal rule.

### 4. Isombe and local-language evidence

**Question:** Should the later review preserve Isombe as an unresolved source conflict until each consultant’s cited evidence and recipe descriptions are compared, instead of choosing a category from the current file set?

**Evidence:** C3 D2 F403-01 places it under Leaf & Vegetable Stews; C4 D2 includes it in both CAT-021 and CAT-022; C5 D1’s summary places it under Core Starches while its D2/D4 place it under Greens/Sauces; C6 D2 FAM-R06 lists Isombe as a synonym for East African Freshwater Fish, while D4 EX-02 lists isambaza and isombe separately. C1/C3/C5 definitions mention different ingredients and preparation contexts.

**Tradeoff:** Keeping the contradiction visible prevents false certainty. The Founder’s direction below clarifies that resolving every regional dispute is not a prerequisite to a workable initial baseline; any unresolved placement can remain documented. No new food research is commissioned by this question.

### 5. Evidence threshold for claims of direct observation

**Question:** For future evaluation work, should claims such as “field study,” “audited,” or a count of menus require a source/observation register in the submission before receiving high evidence-confidence credit?

**Evidence:** C5 claims over 480 verified menus and field studies but provides no source key or observation log; C1/C2 lack resolvable bibliographies; C4 uses named references without detailed links; C3 and C6 provide source registers but do not include all underlying observation artifacts.

**Tradeoff:** Requiring traceable evidence improves comparability but may exclude claims whose underlying collection is not shareable. The present evaluation scores submitted traceability, not truth of the claims.

### 6. Governance choices versus evidence findings

**Question:** Should later reconciliation keep proposals such as “ten merchants trigger a category candidate,” “quarterly review,” custom-category caps and mapping rules in a distinct governance decision section rather than in the candidate classification list?

**Evidence:** C3, C4, C5 and C6 each propose governance mechanisms; C5 gives a numeric ten-merchant threshold without support; C6 explicitly says no external authority governs version/change control. These are design proposals, not demonstrated industry requirements.

**Tradeoff:** Separating governance prevents proposals from appearing as research facts, but Founder decisions will remain open until after the comparative draft.

### 7. Use of the weighted scorecard

**Question:** Should the scorecard be used as a prioritization aid only, with no sole-consultant selection based on its aggregate total?

**Evidence:** Consultants 3 and 6 score 77 and 76, while each has distinct strengths and material gaps. Scorecard entries reflect submission evidence and do not validate external cultural, legal or safety claims.

**Tradeoff:** A score helps surface gaps consistently; treating small numerical differences as rank or authority would overstate the precision of the source record.

## Suggested immediate disposition

1. Accept this evaluation package for Founder review as research only.
2. Decide which of the bounded questions above should govern a later reconciliation exercise.
3. If additional field, language, food-safety or legal evidence is wanted, commission that work separately with explicit scope; none is initiated here.
4. Keep original consultant files and the seven completed assessment outputs unchanged.

No PEWA taxonomy, allergen policy, custom-category governance model, application behavior or foundation decision is approved by this brief.

## Founder disposition — practical baseline direction

The Founder directs the next design phase toward a useful, extensible initial baseline rather than a perfect or final global taxonomy. PEWA classifications are guidance and need not be exhaustive definitions. Businesses should be able to create custom menu categories, collections and suitable classifications, while retaining control over customer-facing names and menu presentation. Shared PEWA classifications and business-created extensions should coexist; where useful, a custom category may map to a shared classification for discovery.

Future additions and refinements should respond to genuine usage and observed gaps. Do not set arbitrary merchant-count thresholds or fixed review cycles now. Maintain explicit safeguards for allergen, dietary and certification claims. Regional terminology disputes should remain documented where unresolved, but resolving every dispute is not a prerequisite to establishing a workable baseline.

This is product-direction guidance for PEWA-F02-R01-C. It does not approve a specific schema, classification inventory, taxonomy, or application implementation. The evaluation’s unresolved-source findings remain evidence records; they need not all be adjudicated before the practical baseline can be drafted.

## PEWA-F02-R01-C handover

The next phase should prepare a practical initial classification and menu-structure baseline informed by the six submissions, alongside business-defined categories and extensions, flexible mappings to shared classifications, multilingual/regional terminology, separation of presentation/classification/discovery, minimal safety-critical claim safeguards, and a usage-led refinement approach. Start with a concise baseline principles and worked-example document; defer exhaustive catalogue enumeration and application architecture.
