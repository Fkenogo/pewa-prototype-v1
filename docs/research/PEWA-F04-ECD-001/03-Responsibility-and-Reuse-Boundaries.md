# Responsibility, Authority and Reuse Boundaries

## Responsibility and authority matrix

| Boundary | Authority and responsibility | PEWA engine responsibility | Explicit limit and evidence gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Participating business | Owns its business identity and relationship with customers; supplies and maintains offering content, prices and availability; chooses what it publishes; decides whether to accept an order; performs food preparation, service, collection and delivery. | Accept and present supplied information faithfully; make product publication/access state visible; accurately record customer submissions and business-declared outcomes. | PEWA must not set or independently warrant business facts, accept an order for a business, or claim fulfillment. In manual engagement the business owns the conversation and resulting commercial decisions. Authority for cancellation, correction, amendment and other exceptions (customer-initiated or business-initiated) is unallocated and needs later definition. |
| PEWA product | Owns the product behavior it performs: validating inputs under approved product rules, enforcing access and organization isolation, publishing business-authorized offerings, providing persistent access/discovery, facilitating manual customer engagement, enforcing the meaning of subscription entitlements for structured ordering, recording enabled selections/orders and communicating recorded states truthfully. | Accountable for integrity, authorization, security and accurate claims within its behavior, and for platform-level handling of reports of misleading or problematic published content and applicable product consumer-protection obligations. | Product responsibility does not establish canonical technical storage ownership, legal data ownership or a Miledge classification. |
| Food-service specialisation (within PEWA) | PEWA remains accountable, as approved product/domain logic, for food-service menu, meal context, service-mode and ordering semantics. The food-service implementation is PEWA's context-specific specialisation of that logic; it is not an independent owner and may not redefine product truth. | Defines and applies those semantics for the approved food/beverage/meal scope and prevents them from being mistaken for universal commerce rules, while preserving the sector-neutral reuse hypothesis (not a Miledge Enduring Capability, Domain or Shared Platform). | No detailed industry logic, workflow or engine decomposition is approved here. Restaurants, cafés, bakeries, confectioneries and hotels remain initial contexts; future institutions are candidates only. |
| Organization configuration | Expresses organization identity and bounded variation within a future approved product strategy. | Provide a bounded ability to apply permitted organization-specific values without silently changing common product meaning. | F05 must set high-level configuration direction. WhatsApp is optional. |

## Business content, pricing, availability, orders and fulfillment

- **Content and catalogue meaning:** the business decides what an offering is and supplies its description and media. PEWA validates and presents what it receives; validation is not independent verification of truth.
- **Pricing:** the business sets prices and commercial terms. PEWA may represent those values faithfully under a future product policy; it does not price or guarantee them.
- **Availability:** the business declares availability. PEWA may publish/reflect that declaration and its own update state. It does not observe kitchen stock or guarantee real-time availability unless separately evidenced and authorized.
- **Order submission:** the customer chooses to submit an order where ordering is enabled. PEWA may record that submission and communicate that it was received by PEWA. Submission is not acceptance.
- **Manual engagement:** PEWA facilitates the mode through offering publication, persistent access and appropriate business contact pathways. The business owns the conversation and the commercial decisions that result; PEWA records no order in this mode. It is separate from, and independent of, optional structured ordering.
- **Ordering gate:** optional structured ordering requires both product entitlement under the business's applicable subscription and business enablement. PEWA is accountable for the product meaning and capability-level enforcement of entitlement; billing mechanism, payment provider, tier pricing and entitlement implementation are unresolved. No subscription plans are designed here.
- **Order acceptance:** only the business makes the acceptance decision. PEWA can record and communicate a business declaration; it cannot infer acceptance from transport delivery, silence or payment alone.
- **Readiness and fulfillment:** the business declares Order Ready and fulfils the order. PEWA reports the declaration as such; it does not independently verify preparation, pickup, table service or delivery.
- **Cancellation, correction and amendment:** not allocated to either party. Customer-initiated and business-initiated actions are both possible; who may initiate, approve or finalise each, and under what safeguards, are capability/authority questions for later definition. No order-state machine or exception workflow is proposed.
- **Reports and consumer protection:** the business is responsible for the accuracy of the content it supplies; that is separate from PEWA's platform accountability to handle reports of misleading or problematic published content and meet applicable product consumer-protection obligations. Detailed reporting, moderation, enforcement and appeals processes are deferred to later requirements and governance.
- **Technical record ownership:** product behavior and record integrity are PEWA responsibilities; business authority over the facts and decisions recorded remains with the business. The canonical technical authority for menus and orders, permitted writers, publication authority at the storage layer and tenancy mechanism remain unresolved under F03.

## Data-class responsibility inventory

This capability-level inventory responds to F03 OD-03. It records responsibility only. It does not assert legal ownership, retention periods, canonical technical authority, database design or storage architecture, and it does not decide the lifecycle or privacy questions listed.

| Data class | Business or actor authority (where established) | Proposed PEWA product responsibility | External/shared dependencies | Unresolved lifecycle and privacy decisions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Business and organisation information | Business owns its identity, relationship with customers and the facts it declares about itself. | Present as supplied; enforce organisation isolation and authorised changes; keep identity clear. | Identity/tenancy candidates are evidence-gated (F02 FD-04); no service adopted. | Correction, export, deletion and retention on closure or exit; organisation/multi-menu semantics; legal ownership. |
| Offering/catalogue records | Business owns content, descriptions, prices, availability and publication choice. | Validate under product rules, preserve identity and publication state, represent faithfully; define food-service semantics as product logic. | Canonical technical authority and writers unresolved (F03 FD-01). | Versioning/history, correction and removal of published offerings, public-copy persistence, retention. |
| Customer information | Customer authority over their own information is not yet established. The business holds the customer relationship; whether PEWA also has a direct customer relationship (guest/account model, recents/favourites) is open. | Protect and use customer data only for approved product purposes; keep it separate from business content; avoid treating a channel identifier as identity by itself (PAP-02). | Identity and messaging candidates are evidence-gated; channel provider handling is external. | Guest/account model; consent and purposes; export, correction, deletion and retention; recents/favourites privacy; role of business versus PEWA as data controller. |
| Selection/order records | Customer originates submissions; business declares acceptance and readiness. Authority over cancellation, correction and amendment unresolved. | Record accurately what PEWA knows (actor, action, status); preserve submission/acceptance/readiness distinctions; protect integrity. | Payment candidates, canonical technical authority and tenancy unresolved. | Retention and audit expectations; access by each party; correction and exception handling; disputes; transaction-integrity obligations. |
| Media | Business owns the media it supplies and its rights to publish it. | Keep content authority and public-versus-protected intent; handle reports of problematic media. | MV-206 evaluation guidance only; no media service or provider adopted. | Rights and takedown, replacement/removal, derived copies, retention, delivery location. |
| Operational events, audit and telemetry data | No business or customer authority established; arises from PEWA operating the product. Business-visible activity records may need separate treatment. | Produce accountable evidence of what PEWA accepted, published and communicated; protect and limit use of event and telemetry data. | Audit, analytics and notification services are candidates only; channel delivery events are transport evidence, not business outcomes. | What is recorded and for whom; personal data in events; access, retention, export and deletion; business access to its own activity; separation of audit from analytics. |

## Sector reuse boundary

| Candidate as reusable | Food-service-specific meaning retained | Evidence required before expansion |
|---|---|---|
| Structured business offerings, publishing, persistent access and discovery | Menu, meal, food/beverage categories, service context, business menu identity and how availability is described | Repeated need across approved initial contexts; stable shared meaning; evidence that the same capability supports more than one implementation without erasing sector meaning |
| Optional structured selection and submission | Food-service order, selected items/modifiers and business response/fulfillment semantics | A credible second context with similar authority and lifecycle; distinct evidence of recurring selection need; no generalized commerce assumptions |
| Bounded organization variation | Organization identity and local presentation or permitted business choices | F05 evidence on which differences can be represented as configuration while retaining common rules |
| Channel-independent product behavior | WhatsApp is the optional intended channel; web/QR are independent access paths | MWA conformance, operational owner, access, support, cost/security conditions and PEWA readiness; evidence for each other shared channel/service separately |

Broader hospitals, schools and corporate cafeterias are future candidates under F01, not a present scope expansion. Evidence for later expansion should establish recurring need, distinct contexts, common capability semantics, actual reuse demand and a bounded fit with business authority. If reuse evidence fails, PEWA can remain a food-service capability set without invalidating the product thesis.
