# Cross-context Consistency and Variation Analysis

F01 includes restaurants, cafés, bakeries, confectioneries, hotels and comparable food-service businesses from the start. Hospitals, schools and corporate cafeterias are future candidates only and are not assumed here. This analysis tests whether the strategy holds across the approved contexts and separates stable shared meaning from bounded variation. It is a hypothesis check, not evidence of cross-sector reuse.

## What stays constant in every context

- Business is primary and identifiable; PEWA is the enabling platform.
- Publication is deliberate; published offerings are public, everything else protected by default.
- Two entitlement-governed modes; ordering needs entitlement and enablement.
- Place Order, Order Accepted and Order Ready keep one meaning and are attributed to their actors; Ready is business-declared.
- Web access is independent; WhatsApp is optional and conditional.
- Truthfulness, isolation, authorization and PEWA report-handling accountability.
- PEWA operates no kitchen, logistics, POS, inventory or fulfilment verification.

## Where contexts legitimately vary

| Context | Likely variation (hypothesis) | Treated as | Strategy test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Restaurant / café | Dine-in and takeaway, multiple menus (for example by time or area), table or counter contact. | Business-declared service context and menus. | Fits configuration; multi-menu semantics open. |
| Bakery / confectionery | Mostly takeaway or pre-order; availability that changes frequently. | Declared availability and ordering ahead. | Fits configuration; availability freshness is open. |
| Hotel | Several outlets, room menus, room service, possible guest-only access, business-managed delivery to a room. | Multiple menus and service contexts described by the business; no PEWA logistics. | Fits configuration, except guest-restricted access, which touches public/protected rules and is flagged. |
| Comparable food-service business | Unknown; evaluated case by case within food, beverage and meal scope. | Case-by-case. | Must pass the admission test below. |

## Admission test for a proposed variation

A difference is **configuration** only if all hold; otherwise it is a product change or out of scope:

1. It reflects a recurring need across more than one approved context, or an unavoidable single-business fact.
2. It changes business-declared information, presentation or a permitted choice, not shared meaning.
3. It requires no new actor authority, status meaning or logistics responsibility.
4. It stays within entitlement and public/protected rules.
5. It needs no organisation-specific code.

Differences failing 2 or 3 go to Product Definition as potential product changes. Differences outside food, beverage and meal scope require a separate Founder scope decision (F01).

## Consistency risks

- **Hotel guest-only access** is an unresolved Product Definition question, not an approved feature; it could force a protected-menu concept the strategy currently treats as exceptional. Business-authorised public offerings remain publicly reachable on the web, and no context is required to publish every offering publicly.
- **Multi-menu organisations** are open and drive publication, discovery and configuration ownership.
- **Time-varying availability** may pressure PEWA toward observing state it has said it will not observe.
- **Pressure toward general commerce** (cross-business basket, delivery tracking) must be refused; it is outside F01.
- **Evidence gap:** all variation above is reasoned, not market-validated.
