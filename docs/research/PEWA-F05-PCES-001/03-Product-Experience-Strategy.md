# Proposed Product Experience Strategy

Direction-level principles. These are not journeys, flows, screens, wireframes, conversation scripts or interface designs. Each principle states intent and a limit so Product Definition can design within it.

| ID | Area | Principle (intent) | Limit and deferred matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| XS-01 | **Business onboarding and readiness** | A business becomes publicly present only when it is identifiable, has something publishable and has been told clearly what it is responsible for (content accuracy, acceptance, preparation, fulfilment). Onboarding should be proportionate: quick to a first useful publication. | What "ready" requires, verification, steps, support and training are for Product Definition. Over-gating small businesses is a recognised risk. |
| XS-02 | **Maintaining and publishing offerings** | The business is the author; PEWA is the publisher of what the business authorises. Publishing is a deliberate act; draft and published states are distinguishable; changes the business makes should be easy and the resulting public state clear and current. | Authoring tools, bulk handling, scheduling, history and correction/removal rules are deferred. PEWA validates; it does not verify truth. |
| XS-03 | **Persistent public menu access** | A published business menu has a stable web destination that works on repeat visits without rescanning, reinstalling or logging in by default. The business is primary and PEWA is visible as the enabling platform. | Business-authorised public offerings stay reachable through independent web destinations; that does not mean every context must publish every offering publicly, since what is published remains the business's choice. Account and guest model, caching, search-index exposure and unpublishing behaviour are open. Whether any context (for example hotel guest-only access) needs restricted access is an unresolved Product Definition question, not an approved feature. No access-control mechanism or identity design is selected. Web access must not depend on any optional channel. |
| XS-04 | **Discovery through PEWA** | PEWA Home gives fast return to recent and optionally saved menus and a route to search; PEWA Directory is separate. Discovery surfaces only what businesses have deliberately published. Any featured placement is clearly labelled, purchased by the business, and never obscures quick return or implies PEWA is the merchant. | Ranking, location, indexing, recents/favourites mechanics and retention, and advertising controls are open. No ratings. |
| XS-05 | **Manual customer engagement** | PEWA makes the offering reachable and provides appropriate ways to contact or engage the business; the customer journey in PEWA can legitimately end there. The business owns the conversation and the commercial decisions. | No conversation flow or script; PEWA records no order in this mode. The approved pathway set is for Product Definition. |
| XS-06 | **Optional structured ordering** | Where the business is entitled and has enabled it, a customer can make selections and submit an order against current published offerings in a predictable way, before arrival where the business allows. It is clearly optional and distinct from manual engagement. | Selection design, modifiers, payment during Place Order (confirmation distinct from acceptance), exceptions and safeguards are open. |
| XS-07 | **Business-declared order outcomes** | Each party sees only what is true: the customer's submission, the business's Accepted, the business's Ready, each attributed to its actor. Ready never implies verified collection or delivery. | Declines, cancellation, correction and amendment (customer- or business-initiated) are unresolved. No state machine or exception workflow is proposed. |
| XS-08 | **Public versus protected information** | Published offerings and business identity are deliberately public. Organisation data, customer information, order records and operational/audit data are protected by default and disclosed only under approved rules. A business can see its own activity; customers see their own. | Protected business, customer and operational information stays protected and is not exposed by public web access. Per-class lifecycle, privacy, retention and access rules are open (F04 inventory). Hotel guest-only or other restricted menu access is an unresolved Product Definition question, not an approved feature. No access-control mechanism, identity design or detailed experience is selected. |

## Cross-cutting experience rules

- **Truthfulness.** Nothing in the experience claims an outcome PEWA has not established; channel delivery is not business acceptance. A change in entitlement never rewrites or misrepresents historical order outcomes (see [document 4](04-Channel-and-Entitlement-Alignment.md)).
- **Platform accountability.** Users can raise a concern about misleading or problematic content and PEWA handles it; the process is deferred.
- **Deterministic first.** Structured, predictable interaction in any automated conversation; AI-assisted interaction deferred and never authoritative for menu facts, prices, availability or orders (F01 §8).
- **Proportion.** Experience effort tracks the approved narrow domain: menus, access, engagement, optional ordering. Not marketplace, POS, kitchen or logistics.
