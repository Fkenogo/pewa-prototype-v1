## Contributions that appear across submissions

### Separate culinary identity from meal role and presentation

Consultants 1, 3, 4, 5 and 6 all articulate some form of separating “what the item is” from a menu heading, occasion, course or role. Consultant 3’s D2 dimensions and Structure Concepts sheet and Consultant 6’s D1 §§3 and 7 provide the clearest explicit models. Consultant 4’s D2/deliverables are a useful counterexample because its report recommends separation while its catalogue places children’s menus, combos and breakfast/brunch among categories.

### Make East African staples and accompaniments visible

All six recognize staples, sauces/stews, greens, brochettes or local names, but Consultant 3 D2 F500/F501/F502/F403 and Consultant 6 D2 FAM-05/FAM-06/FAM-R01/FAM-R02 provide more source-linked catalogue structures. Consultant 4 D2 CAT-020–023 is simpler and easier to inspect. Consultant 1’s C003 and Consultant 2’s CAT-001-A are cautionary examples of how a sparse or malformed structure can preserve or blur regional relevance.

### Preserve synonym value without erasing meaning

Consultants 1, 3, 5 and 6 point to local or multilingual synonyms; Consultants 3 and 6 explicitly note false merges and collisions. Reusable contribution: record both language/region and term scope, retain business display labels, and preserve terms that are not proven equivalent as unresolved. These are proposals, not a validated synonym set.

### Separate safety facts from voluntary claims

Consultants 1, 3, 4, 5 and 6 distinguish at least some food composition facts from diet or certification claims. Consultant 3 D1 §4.E and Consultant 6 D1 §8/D3 TAG-12–26 provide the strongest evidence-centered controls, including unknown/not-declared and cross-contact concepts. Consultant 4 D3 separates peanut/tree nut. Consultant 5’s own D4 examples show why a policy alone is insufficient without item-level recipe evidence.

### Keep multi-location items reusable

All but Consultant 2 at meaningful depth advocate reusing an item across menus/locations while varying price, availability, service point or display. Consultant 3 D1 §4.F and Consultant 6 D1 §9 ground the idea in vendor documentation; Consultants 1, 4 and 5 give useful examples. Governance authority, version lineage and local override limits remain unresolved, as Consultant 6 acknowledges.

## How to reuse these contributions in later work

Reuse should mean “carry forward as a hypothesis or test case with its source and limitation,” not copy a proposed category into a PEWA standard. The next reconciliation stage should preserve original identifiers, map competing proposals side-by-side, mark which evidence is direct versus claimed, and leave each unresolved conflict visible until an authorized decision is made.
