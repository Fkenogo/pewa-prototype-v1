# PEWA-F02-R01-C — Worked Menu Examples

**Status:** Illustrative examples accompanying the Founder-approved v0.1 reference. Business names, section titles, item descriptions and prices below are examples, not observed facts about named businesses. Regional terms are provisional and should be supplied or confirmed by the business.

Each example separates the business’s customer-facing organization from optional shared classifications and from meal context, sellable items, options and safety claims. A shared match is never required for the business to use its own menu category.

## 1. Burundi/Rwanda traditional-food restaurant

**Business menu collection:** `Lunch au Jardin` (business-defined collection)

| Customer-facing category | Example item as the business describes it | Optional shared match | Other dimensions kept separate |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Brochettes du Lac` | Mukeke brochette | FD-03 Grilled, roasted and skewered dishes; FD-08 Fish and seafood dishes if useful | Lunch; may be served as a main or sharing plate; customer chooses staple separately. “Mukeke” remains the business’s term, not an asserted synonym for another fish. |
| `Nos plats locaux` | Isombe with a staple | FD-04 Stews, curries and sauced dishes or FD-09 Vegetable and legume dishes, provisionally | Business description of recipe and ingredients controls. The source conflict in Consultant 6’s Isombe-to-freshwater-fish synonym remains visible; PEWA does not assert a fish equivalence. |
| `Accompagnements` | Matooke / igitoki, as named by the business | FD-05 Starches and staple foods, provisionally | Staple role is contextual, not encoded by the shared food identity. Do not map Matooke to cassava. |
| `À partager` | Isambaza portion | FD-08 Fish and seafood dishes, provisionally | Portion and sharing role are item/menu details. Isambaza is not automatically merged with Mukeke or ndagala. |

The restaurant may instead call the collections “Local Plates,” “Amafunguro y’iwacu,” or another customer-facing name it chooses. It can publish the menu even while Isombe or fish terminology remains unresolved in the shared library.

## 2. Café with coffee, pastries and light meals

**Business collections:** `Coffee Bar`, `All-Day Bites`

| Customer-facing category | Example item | Optional shared match | Other dimensions kept separate |
|---|---|---|---|
| `House Coffee` | Kigali latte | BV-01 Coffee | Available all day; cup size and milk choice are options. “Kigali latte” remains the café’s display name. |
| `Fresh from the Oven` | Almond croissant | FD-15 Pastries and viennoiserie | Breakfast or snack may be its occasion/role. Ingredients and allergen information must come from business-supplied recipe or ingredient records, never from the item name; if no record is supplied, the information remains unknown. |
| `Quick Lunch` | Chicken chapati wrap | FD-11 Sandwiches, burgers and wraps | Lunch; spicy sauce and extra kachumbari are options. |

The same `Kigali latte` item can appear in `House Coffee` on the café menu and in a business collection called `Morning Favourites` on another published menu. The item identity remains the same; presentation and availability can differ by menu or location.

## 4. Hotel with multiple service points

The hotel uses separate business collections for `Terrace Dining`, `Pool Bar` and `Room Service`. A kitchen item can appear in more than one collection without requiring the hotel to adopt PEWA’s labels for its menus.

| Service point and business category | Example item | Optional shared match | Context and variation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Terrace Dining / `Lake & Grill` | Grilled whole fish | FD-03, optionally FD-08 | Dinner; may be a shared plate or main depending on the order. |
| Pool Bar / `From the Grill` | The same grilled whole fish | FD-03, optionally FD-08 | Pool-service presentation and price may differ; availability is local to this menu. |
| Room Service / `Comfort Plates` | Chicken stew with plain rice | FD-04 for the stew; FD-05 for plain rice | Plain rice is a staple/accompaniment here, not a composed rice dish. Late-night availability and room-service collection are separate from food identity. |

The hotel retains its service-point names, item names, menu assignment and availability. The example does not prescribe a corporate master-item system or price inheritance mechanism.

## 5. Business category with no shared equivalent

A small café creates `Auntie’s Fermentation Flights` for a rotating tasting of three house-made drinks with descriptions chosen by the business. The collection and its names are immediately usable in the café’s menu. It may optionally associate individual drinks with BV-08 or BV-09 if the business’s own descriptions fit, but no mapping is required and PEWA does not infer fermentation method or alcohol status.

If the café later observes a useful shared classification gap, it can preserve the exact term and context as a candidate for consideration. The café need not wait for the candidate to be accepted, and no number of other businesses or fixed calendar review is required.

## Safety applied across examples

- If a menu item has unknown or unprovided allergen information, do not display that as allergen-free.
- Peanut and tree-nut information remain separately identifiable.
- Do not infer the ingredient or cross-contact state for an item from the menu name or shared classification.
- Claims such as “gluten-free,” “vegan,” “halal-certified” or “nut-free” are not verified merely because a business uses them as a category, collection or item label. Appropriate business substantiation is needed before presenting them as verified.
