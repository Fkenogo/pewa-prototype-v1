## 1. Purpose, standing and interpretation

### 1.1 Constitutional purpose
This Constitution is PEWA's enduring product-governance charter. It establishes product identity, authority boundaries, durable commitments, accountability and a proportionate method for decisions and amendment. It does **not** establish a legal entity, approve new markets, resolve technical architecture or replace product requirements.

### 1.2 Existing authority remains effective
The approved F01 product thesis and F02–F05 Founder dispositions remain authoritative. This Constitution consolidates their enduring direction and is interpreted consistently with them; it does not silently reopen or supersede them. Where a proposed interpretation would materially alter an approved foundation commitment, the relevant Founder disposition must explicitly authorise that change.

PEWA governs its product within applicable Miledge portfolio and shared-infrastructure authority and applicable FEF engineering controls. Miledge admission, formal classification, Infrastructure Domain or Shared Platform assignment, shared-service existence, operational maturity and ownership are **not** determined here. FEF adoption is **not** implied. Applicable law and external obligations are not displaced by this charter.

### 1.3 Source of truth
The PEWA repository is the canonical project-level record for this Constitution, PEWA decisions and stage state. Miledge and FEF authoritative materials are referenced at their approved versions rather than re-authored as PEWA-controlled rules. If authority appears to conflict, PEWA records the dependency and obtains the relevant authority's determination before making the affected decision.

## 2. Product identity and scope — CP-01, CP-02, CP-05

### CP-01 — Enduring identity
PEWA provides dependable product capabilities for structured offerings publication, persistent customer access and permitted customer engagement, selection or ordering. PEWA is a product with accountable domain behaviour, not merely a website, channel integration or isolated messaging bot. PEWA's product capability responsibility does not automatically imply ownership of every underlying service, technical record or infrastructure layer.

### CP-02 — Approved initial market
Food, beverages and meal-related offerings are PEWA's **only currently approved initial product domain**, serving the food-service contexts described in F01, including restaurant, café, bakery, confectionery and hotel food/beverage menus. PEWA is not presently authorised as an unrestricted goods-and-services marketplace or general e-commerce platform. Wider structured-offerings applicability is a working, commercially unvalidated hypothesis, not approved market expansion.

### CP-05 — Evidence-led reuse and controlled evolution
PEWA should reuse common capabilities across demonstrated comparable needs. Variations must be justified against approved product requirements and the F05 configuration admission principles; speculative generalisation must not drive unnecessary complexity. Routine evolution within approved commitments can be delegated. Material expansion in industry, purpose, accountability, operating model or core commercial direction requires explicit Founder approval and applicable Miledge authority; no product-only decision may classify PEWA within the portfolio.

## 3. Product commitments and business relationship — CP-03, CP-04

### CP-03 — Business-first commercial relationship
Each participating business remains the identifiable provider of the offerings presented through its menu. It controls the correctness of its supplied descriptions, prices, availability and images, business acceptance and readiness declarations, customer relationship, preparation and physical fulfilment. PEWA must present the business as the provider and must not impersonate the merchant.

PEWA controls and is accountable for truthful product interpretation, validation and presentation, correct platform handling of enabled interactions, entitlement meaning, security and other platform obligations. Technical custody or publication of business-supplied records does not transfer business authority over the content to PEWA; canonical technical authority and write/publish mechanisms require subsequent decisions. Business autonomy does not prevent PEWA from exercising proportionate platform safeguards when problematic content, misuse or legal duties require action.

### CP-04 — Persistent web access and channel independence
Intentionally public business menus must be independently reachable through stable web destinations. A customer need not repeatedly scan a QR code or launch WhatsApp to revisit a public menu. PEWA Home and a separate Directory remain F01-approved experience directions; detailed discovery and persistence mechanisms are deferred.

WhatsApp, QR and other channels are optional or complementary entry/interaction routes; no single external channel defines PEWA's domain meaning. The intended Miledge WhatsApp Adapter remains a **conditional** shared dependency requiring demonstrated conformance, availability, support, operational ownership and integration readiness before adoption. Provider selection or integration is not authorised by this charter. Optional-channel failure must not eliminate the independent web-access commitment.

### 3.1 Protected operating modes
Both F04-approved modes remain part of PEWA's product direction: (1) published-offerings access with business-controlled manual engagement, potentially ending outside PEWA without an order record; and (2) optional structured ordering, available only where both subscription entitlement and business enablement apply. The latter reflects customer order placement and business-declared acceptance/readiness; declared readiness must not be misrepresented as independently verified physical fulfilment. Exceptions and safeguards need further product definition. Subscription tiers, prices, limits and operational policies are not approved here.

## 4. Decision authority and delegation — CP-06 to CP-09

### CP-06 — Founder product authority
The Founder holds final PEWA product authority over enduring identity, Stage 1–6 initiation/disposition/closure, material scope or commercial direction, approved product-level baselines, constitutional amendments and separate engineering authorisation, **within** Miledge reserved matters, applicable FEF constraints and law. A PEWA Founder disposition is not a substitute for a reserved Miledge decision.

### CP-07 — Bounded delegation
The Founder may explicitly delegate routine product and execution decisions within approved scope, including to appointed product leads, authorised contributors and engineering agents. Delegation must identify its purpose and limits in an appropriate existing project record. It never implicitly includes authority to redefine PEWA's purpose, enter a new approved market, overturn material decisions, admit or classify a Miledge capability, select a reserved shared-service owner, bypass an independent stage gate, or commence unapproved engineering. Executing an agreed decision is different from creating a new material one.

### CP-08 — Reserved authority and escalation
PEWA decides product identity, definition, requirements, domain semantics, entitlement meaning and product-level choices. Participating businesses decide their supplied commercial facts and declared business outcomes. Miledge alone decides admission/classification, Domains, Shared Platforms, shared-service existence/ownership/contracts and reserved portfolio architecture. FEF supplies engineering-governance reference and, when separately adopted/applicable, engineering work-package discipline; it does not itself authorise implementation. Providers operate their contracted services but do not determine PEWA's product meaning.

Escalate only when a proposed decision materially implicates reserved authority, a binding external constraint or a real dependency. Record the dependency, do not presume an answer, and continue unrelated authorised work. Material changes to approved Miledge or FEF authority are reconciled before the dependent decision; immaterial updates do not hold up PEWA.

### CP-09 — Proportionate, traceable decisions
Material product decisions should identify the question, responsible authority, outcome, concise rationale, effective scope, affected prior decisions and unresolved dependencies. The project repository is the record; routine implementation and editorial changes may use ordinary PR/work-package evidence. Avoid duplicative approval paperwork or minor non-blocking governance cycles. Separate Founder authority remains required at **each** project-home stage's start, review/disposition and closure. No stage starts by inference from an earlier closure.

## 5. Accountability and safeguards — CP-10 to CP-12

### CP-10 — Product and historical event integrity
Commercial, entitlement, configuration or channel changes must not silently falsify, erase the meaning of or misrepresent recorded orders, business declarations or historical product outcomes. A business-declared event must remain distinguishable from an independently verified event. Legitimate corrections, cancellations, amendments, retention actions, erasure and legally required changes must remain possible under subsequently approved, accountable rules; preservation of truth does not require indefinite retention of personal information. Specific order-exception authority, active-order treatment upon entitlement changes and technical audit controls are deferred.

### CP-11 — Public publication versus protected information
PEWA must support intentional public discovery and access to published offerings while protecting private organisation and customer information from unauthorised disclosure. Public menu publication does not make administration or customer records public. Protected hotel/guest use cases, account mechanics, role permissions, privacy rights, retention and data lifecycle require later decisions; this principle selects no tenancy or identity mechanism.

### CP-12 — Accountability follows control and obligation
PEWA is accountable for platform functions, accurate outcome communication, product integrity, applicable privacy, security, consumer-protection duties and proportionate mechanisms for reporting or responding to harmful, inaccurate or misleading content and platform conduct. Participating businesses remain accountable for their supply of content, their decisions and physical fulfilment. Neither party's responsibilities eliminate applicable shared or statutory obligations. Detailed enforcement, appeals, remedies, response targets, jurisdiction-specific terms and consumer-dispute procedures belong to later authorised work.

## 6. Governance, amendment and supersession — CP-13, CP-14

### CP-13 — Controlled amendments
An amendment to PEWA's enduring purpose, constitutional authority structure, core product commitments or foundational safeguards requires a distinct, explicit Founder disposition that identifies the affected clauses and rationale, and is recorded in the PEWA repository. If it implicates reserved Miledge authority or applicable FEF constraints, obtain the appropriate separate determination before effect. Material product decisions within this Constitution need Founder approval but do not automatically require a constitutional amendment; routine improvements stay within delegated scope.

### CP-14 — Explicit authorised supersession
A later document is not superior merely because it is newer. A material amendment or replacement must identify what earlier decision or provision it supersedes, by whose authority and to what extent. Preserve history and traceability. In case of uncertainty, maintain the last valid approved baseline while seeking clarification from the competent authority; do not silently infer new powers or change business-event meaning.

### 6.1 Stage control
The six-stage project-home sequence in the PEWA Project-Home Programme remains controlling: (1) Constitution, (2) Product Definition, (3) Requirements, (4) Product Architecture and technical decisions, (5) FEF adoption/readiness confirmation, (6) separately authorised Engineering. Only Stage 1 has received a Founder start instruction as of this draft. Drafting and a PR are **not** constitutional approval, Stage 1 closure or Stage 2 initiation. Engineering, provider selection and deployment remain unauthorised.

## 7. Continuity, application and reserved decisions

### 7.1 Application
Once separately approved, this Constitution will guide subsequent PEWA product decisions and be used to test proposed changes against enduring product commitments. It is not an implementation specification, external contract, privacy policy or jurisdiction-specific legal opinion. Interpret it with the existing F01–F05 approvals; record any material departure through the appropriate Founder and/or Miledge authority.

### 7.2 Expressly unresolved
This charter **does not decide** canonical menu/order technical authority, write/publish ownership, tenant implementation, data-controller or legal ownership assignments, customer-data access and lifecycle, subscription plans or lapse/grace/suspension/active-order treatment, cancellations/corrections/amendments, payment safeguards, guest-only hotel access, multi-menu semantics, reporting/moderation/appeals processes, MWA readiness/integration, other shared services, providers, deployment architecture or FEF readiness. All **26 items** in the PEWA-PHT-001 handover allocation remain open unless later explicitly disposed of by competent authority. Later-stage reallocations may be approved without reopening the transition, provided no authority or previously approved commitment is silently displaced.

### 7.3 Review and effect
**Founder approval recorded 16 October 2026.** The Founder approved this Constitution and instructed continuation. Controlled repository merge makes this the accepted PEWA constitutional text. Stage 1 closure remains a separate action; Stage 2 needs an independent Founder start instruction.
