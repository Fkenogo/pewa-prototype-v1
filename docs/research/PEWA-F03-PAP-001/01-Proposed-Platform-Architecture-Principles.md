# PEWA-F03-PAP-001 — Proposed Platform Architecture Principles

**Status:** Proposed; pending Founder review. These principles do not select a design, provider or hosting environment.

| ID | Proposed principle | Rationale and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| PAP-01 | **Organisation is the primary business boundary.** Preserve clear separation among organisations and their offerings, business decisions and data. A business remains the identifiable provider and authority over its supplied content, prices and availability. | F01 §§2, 7, 11; F02 FD-02. This does not define organisation hierarchy or claim that each organisation maps to a particular technical tenant. |
| PAP-02 | **Authorize by domain authority, not identity alone.** Authentication establishes an actor; PEWA product rules must determine whether that actor may act for an organisation or perform a business action. Channel identifiers or possession of a phone number are not, by themselves, business authority. | MPA-002 authority separation; MTAIP-001 §7; MV-207 §3; F02 responsibility boundary. Detailed identities, roles and permissions remain out of scope. |
| PAP-03 | **Isolate organisations securely; defer the tenancy mechanism.** Prevent unauthorized disclosure of protected organisation data and unauthorized access, with least privilege across access paths. Permit cross-organisation publication of offerings explicitly designated public under approved product/business controls. Select a shared or product-specific tenant mechanism only after requirements, service ownership and controls are evidenced. | MPA-002 §3.8 and authority boundaries; MTAIP-001 §§4, 7; MV-206 §2.5; F01 §5 requires directory discovery of participating businesses. “Multi-tenant” in F01 is a product thesis, not a tenancy design. |
| PAP-04 | **Separate business information authority from product record responsibility.** Businesses control the meaning and correctness of supplied offerings. PEWA is responsible for any validation and presentation it performs and for truthful product behavior. Do not imply PEWA independently verifies food quality, availability or fulfilment. | F01 §§2, 7; F02 FD-02. The authoritative technical record, write path and final menu/order state ownership are unresolved. |
| PAP-05 | **Make data lifecycle explicit before persistence decisions.** Later decisions must account for export, correction, deletion, retention, privacy, audit and portability obligations for each relevant data class. Preserve business control and practical portability where required; do not duplicate a canonical store solely for hypothetical migration. | MTAIP-001 §§4–6; MV-206 §§1–2; F01 §§7–8. Retention periods, legal roles and deletion semantics require later evidence and authority. |
| PAP-06 | **Keep reusable structured offerings separate from food-service rules.** Evaluate sector-neutral publishing, persistent access, discovery and optional structured selection as a capability hypothesis. Keep PEWA's approved initial food/beverage/meal scope and F01 exclusions intact; do not turn reuse into general commerce. | F01 §§3–4, 10; F02 FD-01; MPA-002 §§3.1–3.2; MV-303 §§2, 7. No Miledge capability or portfolio classification is decided here. |
| PAP-07 | **Prefer configuration before bespoke variation.** Where a later-approved product capability supports organisation variation, express it through bounded configuration where practical. Keep sector rules explicit and do not let configuration silently expand the shared product domain. | MPA-002 §3.5; MV-303 §2; F01 §§4, 6, 10. No configuration model or workflow is specified. |
| PAP-08 | **Products own domain meaning; shared services own only their approved boundary.** PEWA retains product meaning for offerings, discovery and enabled order outcomes. A shared transport or service may handle its approved technical concern without becoming authoritative for PEWA business outcomes. | F01 §8; F02 FD-03/04; MPA-002 §§4.2, 5.2; MV-207; MWA-001/002. Final layer and state ownership remain subject to authority and verified contracts. |
| PAP-09 | **Integrate through versioned, verified contracts.** Before depending on a shared service, establish its approved contract/version, accountable owner, conformance evidence, operational state, support and access path. Keep product behavior independent of undocumented implementation details. | MPA-002 §§2–3.8; MPA-005 §§3.2, 4; F02 FD-03/04. Contract approval alone does not establish deployability or readiness. |
| PAP-10 | **Shared channels are optional and failure-contained.** WhatsApp use remains conditional on Miledge authority and separately verified readiness. A channel or shared-service outage must not make the independent web/QR access path unavailable; product outcomes must not be represented as successful merely because transport accepted a message. | F01 §§5, 8; F02 FD-03; MV-207 §§1, 4; MWA-001/002. Does not specify retry, queue, timeout or UX behavior. |
| PAP-11 | **Let approved requirements drive infrastructure; preserve proportionate portability.** Keep durable domain authority in product-owned meaning/interfaces when provider dependency would create material control, migration, security, cost or continuity risk. Accept provider-specific implementation where switching cost is proportionate; avoid speculative abstraction and duplicate authorities. | MTAIP-001 §§2–6, 8; MV-206 §§1–3. No database, provider, hosting or portability mechanism is selected. |
| PAP-12 | **Protect data and operate with accountable evidence.** Apply least privilege, data minimization, appropriate privacy controls, auditable material business actions, failure isolation, recovery planning and explicit operational ownership to later-approved architecture. Distinguish platform security and consumer obligations from business fulfilment responsibility. | F01 §§7, 11; MPA-002 §3.8 and §§10–13; MTAIP-001 §§9–12; MV-206 §2. No security design, retention schedule, SLO or operational runbook is specified. |

## Interpretation

These are principles for later decisions, not a proposed component diagram. “Product-owned” identifies responsibility for domain meaning and authority only; it does not decide where records reside, which service writes them, or their Miledge layer. F01/F02 authority and Miledge source status are summarized in the [traceability matrix](02-Authority-and-Constraint-Traceability.md). Open questions and decision stages are listed in [Unresolved Decisions](05-Unresolved-Decisions-and-Stage-Allocation.md).
