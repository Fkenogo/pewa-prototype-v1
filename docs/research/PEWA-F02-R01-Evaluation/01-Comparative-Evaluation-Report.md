# PEWA-F02-R01-B — Comparative Evaluation Report

**Status:** Research evaluation for Founder review. This is not an approved PEWA classification standard and does not consolidate consultant proposals.

## Authority and method

The governing reference is [00-Evaluation-Scope-and-Criteria-Reconstruction.md](00-Evaluation-Scope-and-Criteria-Reconstruction.md). It distinguishes reconstructed historical scope from Founder-authorised scoring methodology. The original research brief was not recovered; this evaluation uses only the eight A–H scope labels and the six criteria authorized there. Original consultant submissions are primary evidence of what was delivered. The seven PEWA-F02-R01 assessment outputs were used for file inventory, extraction, source-line provenance and known exception tracking.

All 21 consultant files in the research folder were reviewed at the narrative, table and workbook-sheet level. For workbooks, catalogue rows and dimensions were examined in the submitted sheets; for embedded catalogues, source lines were considered alongside the parsed assessment inventory. Source references were not treated as verified merely because they resolve to a URL. No additional external research was performed. “Evidence confidence” below describes confidence in the evaluation finding based on the submitted material, not confidence that a consultant’s underlying cultural, regulatory or market claim is true.

The weights are applied as: **Weighted total = SUM(score/5 × criterion weight)**. Scores measure submission evidence, not taxonomy correctness as adjudicated by an outside authority. Scores do not remove the separate reporting of missing deliverables, unsupported claims, safety issues and regional classification concerns.

## Consultant-by-consultant assessment

### Consultant 1

The RTF report is unusually clear about the distinction between what, when, how and where an item is presented. It covers A–H, proposes Core + Extension governance, and illustrates local staples, café/bakery/bar needs, item reuse and synonyms (D1 §§A–H; D4). Its D2/D3 are eight rows apiece, not comprehensive catalogues. In D2, C003 and C004 have unquoted comma-containing fields and parse as 19 and 16 fields against a 14-field header. D3 T002 `Contains_Nuts` treats peanuts and tree nuts as one allergen field, while D1 names them separately in its US allergen list. Numeric citation markers such as `[[23]]` do not resolve to a bibliography in the submission. Findings are useful as conceptual prompts, but the catalogue and source evidence are insufficient to substantiate broad claims.

### Consultant 2

The report presents a faceted view of item, occasion, course and characteristics, and emphasizes local names, restaurant archetypes and menu reuse. The actual D2 sample contains seven rows and explicitly says the full catalogue has 200+ rows; D3 contains five rows. The claimed full catalogue is not a submitted record set and cannot earn completeness credit. `CAT-001-A` groups “Matooke/Cassava” as “Tubers,” while the same report describes Matooke as green banana and maps Igitoke/Matooke to green banana (D1 §§2–3; D2). That is an internal classification ambiguity, not an externally adjudicated botanical ruling. The report gives no resolvable source register or URLs. Governance and multi-location concepts remain high-level.

### Consultant 3

Consultant 3 supplies a detailed 165-row D2 plus separate Course & Role, Meal Occasions, Cuisines, Structure Concepts and Sources sheets. Its strongest conceptual contribution is to explicitly separate dish form, role, occasion, cuisine and attributes, then keep business menu presentation separate (D1 §§4.A, 4.D; D2 sheets). The regional catalogue includes dish groups such as F403-01 Isombe/Sombe/Pondu/Saka-Saka and F502-01 Matooke/Igitoki; the report spans African regions and marks weak or missing evidence. D1 discusses food-safety distinctions and cross-contact handling, but the referenced 52-tag D3 was not submitted, so no structured Consultant 3 allergen records or separate peanut/tree-nut catalogue fields are available for evaluation (D1 §1, §4.E, §16; completeness matrix). Source traceability is comparatively strong: D2 has source IDs and the `Sources` sheet has 81 entries; a high source count does not establish source quality or independent verification. Native-speaker validation, Burundi menu structure and local search behavior remain open (D1 §§2, 6).

### Consultant 4

Consultant 4 provides all five deliverables separately and gives eight clear archetype examples (D1; D4). Its “Staples / Swallow,” “Stews & Sauces,” “Leafy Greens” and other regional entries preserve East African meal structure; the D3 explicitly separates peanut and tree-nut allergens and marks factual versus declared values (D2 CAT-020–025; D3 TAG-005–008). Its catalogue also classifies Children’s Menu, Combos & Meal Deals and Breakfast/Brunch as categories, and places some regional categories beside course-oriented categories at the top level. The reconciliation report acknowledges these overlaps but does not resolve the underlying level/type inconsistency (D2 CAT-050–053; D5 §§1–3). Evidence is primarily secondary; its “East African high evidence” claim is qualified in its own report by the absence of primary physical-menu observation and reliance on culinary/travel references (D1 §2; D5 §5). References often identify publications or standards without URLs or record-level source mapping.

### Consultant 5

Consultant 5’s single Markdown document integrates D1–D5 and is detailed across A–H, specialist businesses, 8 menu archetypes, facets, multi-location controls and governance. Its strongest reusable ideas are its explicit safe-harbor states, separate trust bands, multilingual search, and scenario-based testing of complex menus. The quality limitations are material: the report asserts field research and digital audits across 42 sectors and “over 480 verified menus” (D1 methodology), but supplies no source key, observation log or auditable menu corpus. The report says all 14 major allergens use a “three-value” model, while D3 contains nine TG-AL rows and four states (`contains`, `free_from_certified`, `may_contain`, `undeclared`) (D1 §E; D3). In D4, the Isombe example is labeled both “Nut-Free” and “Dairy-Free,” although the report describes Isombe with peanut paste; that specific scenario has no recipe evidence (D1 §B; D4 example 2). Another example labels a dish Halal Certified without showing the claimed certification record (D4 example 1). The D2 raw lines include four malformed records (CL-FD-06, CL-FD-07, CL-FD-08, CL-BV-05); parsed interpretations are separate from preserved raw lines in the assessment inventory. D1 places Isombe among “Core Starches / Staples,” but D2 CL-FD-05 and D4 place it under Traditional Greens/Leafy Greens (D1 Executive Summary; D2 line 450; D4 example 2).

### Consultant 6

Consultant 6 provides the original RTF D1 and recovered D2–D5 companions, including 63 D2 rows, 58 D3 rows, 8 menu examples and a 36-row source key (assessment inventory; D1 §16; D2/D3/D4 workbooks). Its report is particularly candid about source classes, evidential limits, East African menu-structure gaps and the absence of an external standard for governance decisions (D1 §§2, 5, 9–10, 15; D5). The source key provides numbered URLs, and D1 citations resolve to those entries; some are broad organizational pages, secondary sources or time-sensitive menu links. The source key does not provide the claimed raw menu corpus, and sources have not been independently verified. D2’s 40 rows typed `Family` include service, commercial and content notions (e.g., Breakfast, Brunch, Children’s Meals); it does not classify combos as a bundle category: NOTE-01 explicitly describes combos and meal deals as composite sellable items, not categories. Its FAM-10 is Meat & Poultry. The 19 regional `Category` rows are not always parented under a semantically matching family (e.g., FAM-R01 “East African Staples” is parented to FAM-06 “Stews, Curries & Sauces”; D2 rows 7 and 42). FAM-R06 lists Isombe as a synonym for “East African Freshwater Fish,” while D4 EX-02 lists isambaza and isombe as distinct menu items—an internally contradictory source mapping that must be carried as a regional synonym error candidate. These records make the package useful comparative evidence but not a settled model.

## Six outstanding PR #2 findings examined

The following findings are evaluated as source issues, not resolved taxonomy decisions. Their originating evidence is also recorded in assessment register ISS-002 and ISS-024–ISS-028.

1. **Consultant 1 malformed D2 lines:** C003 and C004 (original RTF lines 89–90) contain extra unquoted delimiters, yielding 19 and 16 parsed fields against 14 headers. The surrounding content remains readable, but columns cannot be silently treated as reliable structured data.
2. **Consultant 5 allergen coverage:** D1 asserts “all 14 major allergens” with a three-value state; D3 includes nine TG-AL entries and four listed enum states. It is an internal completeness/terminology mismatch. The omitted groups cannot be filled from outside research in this assessment.
3. **Consultant 1 source references:** Numeric markers such as `[[2]]`, `[[23]]` and `[[41]]` have no supplied bibliography/source key. These are unresolved citations, not verified evidence.
4. **Peanuts versus tree nuts:** Consultant 1’s D1 enumerates them separately but D3 T002 combines them as `Contains_Nuts`. Consultants 4, 5 and 6 provide separate peanut and tree-nut fields in their submitted catalogues; Consultant 3’s D1 discusses allergen handling, but its referenced D3 is missing and no structured allergen rows are available. Consultant 6 also has an aggregate `Nut-free` claim. Separate allergen identity is inconsistently represented in the available structured evidence. The correctness of a particular item’s allergen state still requires recipe and cross-contact evidence.
5. **Matooke placement:** Consultant 2’s CAT-001-A combines Matooke and Cassava under Tubers. Its own D1 elsewhere calls Matooke green banana; Consultants 1, 3 and 5 also describe it as a cooking banana/plantain preparation. This internal evidence conflict supports flagging the combined assignment as ambiguous. It does not substitute for local-language and culinary validation.
6. **Isombe placement and synonym mapping:** Consultant 5 includes Isombe in D1’s “Core Starches / Staples,” but D2 CL-FD-05 and D4 example 2 assign it to Traditional Greens/Vegetable Sauces. Consultant 3 places the Isombe dish group under F403 Leaf & Vegetable Stews; Consultant 4 includes it in both CAT-021 Stews & Sauces and CAT-022 Leafy Greens. Consultant 6 D2 FAM-R06 lists `Isombe` as a synonym of “East African Freshwater Fish,” while D4 EX-02 lists isambaza and isombe as distinct menu items. These competing assignments and the C6 synonym contradiction are preserved as source evidence; no single classification is selected.

## Evidence confidence and boundaries

High-confidence findings here concern what the submitted files contain: record counts, source-key presence, stated limitations and internal contradictions. Confidence is moderate for comparative conclusions about research quality because underlying URLs, menus, legal instruments and fieldwork claims were not independently checked. Claims about applicable law, botanical/culinary identity, national dishes, religious certification, allergen safety and actual consumer search behavior remain consultant claims or questions unless directly supported by a submitted primary record. No citations have been upgraded to “verified” solely based on their presence.

The evaluation finds promising reusable approaches, but it does not approve a taxonomy, resolve a cultural or legal question, or authorize additional field research. Missing D3 from Consultant 3, missing full catalogues claimed by Consultant 2, weak evidence traceability from Consultants 1, 2, 4 and 5, and region-specific validation needs remain material comparative limitations.
