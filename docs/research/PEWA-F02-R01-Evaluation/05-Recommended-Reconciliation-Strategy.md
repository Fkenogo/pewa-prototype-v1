# PEWA-F02-R01-B — Recommended Reconciliation Strategy

**Status:** Proposed method and comparison direction only. This document does not create, approve or consolidate a taxonomy.

## Recommended direction

Use a **source-first, dimension-separated comparison process** for the later reconciliation draft. Begin with the areas of agreement across submissions—especially separating culinary identity from occasion, course/role, business presentation, attributes and commercial bundles—then compare the competing catalogue structures without selecting one consultant as the winner.

For the working comparison baseline, use Consultant 3’s explicit category/dimension sheets and Consultant 6’s source-keyed report and gap register as complementary reference frames. Use Consultant 4’s regional category examples, Consultant 5’s specialist and multi-location scenarios, and Consultants 1–2’s local-name and Core/Extension proposals as counterexamples and candidate contributions. This is a recommendation about **how to organize the review**, not an endorsement of their proposed labels or standards. Consultant 3’s missing D3 and Consultant 6’s hierarchy inconsistencies must remain visible throughout.

## Proposed sequence for a later authorized reconciliation

### 1. Freeze evidence provenance before comparing labels

Maintain each source’s consultant ID, file, sheet/section, source row/line and original identifier. Keep workbook and CSV mirrors distinguishable while identifying them as one logical deliverable pair where appropriate. Keep Consultant 5’s verbatim lines and parsed interpretations separate. Treat source-key rows and URLs as reference occurrences, not verified sources.

### 2. Compare semantic dimensions before category trees

First map submitted concepts as one of: food/beverage identity, course or service role, meal occasion, cuisine/region, menu presentation, sellable item, modifier, bundle, attribute/claim, or availability. Preserve a “consultant’s own type” column. Do not silently convert C4 “Combo” or C6 “Family” into another type.

### 3. Build a conflict table, not a merged catalogue

For every overlapping or disputed label, retain the source identifiers side-by-side and record: agreement, contradiction, missing evidence, source confidence, safety relevance and a bounded decision question. Start with the high-impact conflicts in [04-Conflict-and-Gap-Register.md](04-Conflict-and-Gap-Register.md): staples/roles, Isombe, Matooke, bundles, allergen states, peanut/tree-nut separation and synonyms that may collide.

### 4. Apply evidence confidence independently of consultant confidence language

Use evidence classes grounded in the submitted material: directly observed item/menu; first-party platform documentation; standard/regulatory text cited by consultant; secondary culinary/travel reference; consultant interpretation; unsupported/uncorroborated claim. A source label such as “High” or “OBS-DIR” does not upgrade a claim when no corresponding record or source can be traced.

### 5. Keep safety and regional checks as blocking review dimensions

Before any later taxonomy decision, separately review allergen identities and status states, ingredient/modifier inheritance, cross-contact, and business-declared dietary/certification claims. Do not derive “free from” from blank, absent or unknown data. Separately check regional terms and placements with traceable local-language evidence; preserve uncertain and same-name-different-food cases rather than forcing synonym merges.

### 6. Record alternatives and tradeoffs

For each unresolved item, state at least two source-supported alternatives where they exist. Example: treat staple as culinary category plus a placement-specific role, or as a dedicated region-specific family. Compare implications for navigation, discovery, reusability and cultural fit, then leave the decision open for Founder direction. Do not infer which one is correct from record count or weighted score alone.

## Alternatives and tradeoffs

### Consultant-led starting point

**Alternative:** Choose one consultant’s structure as the initial draft and reconcile later. This can move quickly and keeps internal consistency, but it risks importing missing evidence, contradictory taxonomy levels, unresolved safety fields or unverified regional terms. The scorecard offers no defensible basis for sole-source adoption.

### Composite, evidence-led starting point (recommended)

**Alternative:** Organize by shared dimensions, compare source-supported regional concepts across C3/C4/C6, and use C1/C2/C5 to test edge cases and business scenarios. This retains diverse contributions and makes contradictions visible, but requires more deliberate issue tracking and Founder decisions.

### Defer disputed concepts

Where a source conflict cannot be resolved by supplied evidence, keep the concept as a candidate/unresolved comparison rather than inventing a canonical answer. This reduces false precision but leaves gaps in a later draft until Founder directs further work.

## Decisions this strategy does not make

- Which consultant or catalogue becomes canonical.
- Whether any category is approved, merged, retired or renamed.
- Which jurisdiction’s allergen list governs PEWA.
- Which certification authorities PEWA recognizes.
- Which regional labels or synonyms are valid.
- Whether to commission fieldwork or legal/regulatory research.
