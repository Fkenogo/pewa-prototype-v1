# PEWA-F02-R01-C — Practical Classification Baseline v0.1

**Status:** **PEWA-F02-R01-C v0.1 — Founder Approved.** Approved as a practical initial reference, not an exhaustive or mandatory taxonomy, product schema, or implementation decision.

## Purpose and authority

This document describes the approved useful initial shared classification reference for food and beverages in PEWA menus, based on the verified repository baseline `main@e48cb82184b3b421484e318dacc9f0ba7d207f64`. It gives businesses optional common labels that support reuse and discovery while preserving local menu control. The target is a reasonable starting point that can improve through observed usage and gaps, not a complete or permanently fixed global taxonomy.

F01 is Founder-approved and defines PEWA as reusable menu publishing and ordering infrastructure for food, beverages and meal-related offerings. It keeps businesses responsible for their menu content and service. The Foundation Programme says F02 is pending and no implementation is authorised. This package records the approved R01 v0.1 practical reference; it does not change F01 or authorise implementation or jurisdiction-specific regulatory policy.

Inputs include the six consultant submissions and the completed comparative evaluation. The evaluation identified useful patterns—especially separating culinary identity from presentation, meal occasion and role—and material gaps and contradictions. Consultant labels below are candidate inputs, not verified facts. No new external research was conducted. Source references point to submitted files; cited sources and regional equivalences have not been independently verified.

## Principles

1. **Useful shared guidance:** Offer a modest set of recognizable food and beverage classifications. Businesses may use, rename for display, combine, extend, or ignore them.
2. **Business menu control:** A business controls its customer-facing menu names, categories, collections, sequence and presentation.
3. **No single rigid tree:** A food identity, how it was prepared, its role in a meal and where the business places it answer different questions. Do not force them into one mandatory hierarchy.
4. **Optional mapping:** A business-created category may map to one or more shared classifications when that helps discovery. A mapping is optional, may be broad or absent, and does not replace the business label.
5. **Regional humility:** Preserve regional names and spelling as supplied by the business. Treat uncertain terms and equivalences as provisional; do not make a disputed synonym a required mapping.
6. **Grows through use:** Add shared classifications when real menu use shows a recurring gap. Do not impose merchant-count thresholds, fixed review cycles, or automatic promotion rules.
7. **Safety claims are distinct:** Business menu labels, ingredient facts, allergen information and dietary/certification claims are different kinds of statements. A custom label does not make a claim verified.

## Eight separate menu dimensions

| Dimension | Question it answers | Examples | Baseline treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Food or beverage identity | What kind of food or drink is offered, and what preparation may be useful in its shared label? | Stew, grilled fish, coffee, pastry | Optional match to a shared reference entry in Catalogue 02. Some entries use a preparation as a practical browsing class; no universal preparation hierarchy is implied. |
| Business menu category or collection | Where does this business choose to show it? | “Local plates,” “Poolside,” “Amahoro favourites” | Business-defined, customer-facing presentation. A collection may group categories or items. |
| Meal occasion | When is it available or intended? | Breakfast, brunch, lunch, late-night | Availability or meal context; not required to be a food classification. |
| Course or meal role | How does it function in this meal? | Starter, main, staple, relish, accompaniment, dessert | Contextual role; the same item can have different roles. |
| Sellable item and composite item | What does the customer select and buy? | One plate; a set meal with included choices | A sellable composite is an item made up of components, not automatically a food category. |
| Modifiers and options | What can the customer add, remove or choose? | Extra kachumbari, spice level, milk choice | Selection options attached to an item or composite. |
| Attributes and declarations | What factual or business-declared properties are stated? | Served cold, contains peanuts, vegan claim | Keep fact, unknown status and business claim distinguishable. Apply safety boundaries in this document. |
| Search and discovery terminology | What words may help find it? | Menu name, local spelling, translated term | Preserve the business wording; add only scoped, confidence-labelled synonyms. |

### Example of separation

One grilled fish item may have the shared identity “grilled fish”; appear in a business collection called “Lake favourites”; be available at lunch; serve as a main in one meal and a shared plate in another; and offer fries or plantain as selectable options. Those are related facts, but they are not all parent-child food categories.

## Minimal safety boundaries

- Keep **peanuts** and **tree nuts** distinguishable wherever allergen information is represented.
- Unknown, missing or unprovided allergen information must never mean “does not contain.”
- Do not infer ingredients, allergen presence/absence, or cross-contact conditions from a dish name, category, cuisine, recipe stereotype, or another item.
- Keep “contains,” “may contain/cross-contact,” and “unknown or not provided” conceptually distinct. This proposal does not define a universal legal set, required jurisdictional disclosure, or customer-facing regulatory policy.
- Dietary, religious, organic, “free-from,” and certification statements remain business claims and require appropriate substantiation before they can be presented as verified.
- A business-created category or display name is not automatically an ingredient, dietary, safety, or certification attribute.

These are baseline safeguards only. Item-level recipe evidence, business responsibility, and applicable jurisdiction-specific obligations require later policy work; this document does not supply that policy.

## Status vocabulary used in Catalogue 02

- **Approved v0.1 reference:** One of the 34 entries approved as an optional, non-exclusive starting reference. It is not exhaustive or mandatory.
- **Provisional regional example:** A submitted regional word or food example whose exact scope or equivalence needs local confirmation. Provisional terms are optional and never required for a business to use the broader classification.
- **Deferred extension:** A useful idea not included in the shared starter set. A business may still use it in its own menu.

## Founder disposition

**PEWA-F02-R01-C v0.1 — Founder Approved**

- The 34-entry starter catalogue is approved as a practical initial reference.
- Shared classifications are optional, non-exclusive and extensible. Businesses may create and publish custom categories, collections and names without central approval; mapping to shared classifications is optional.
- Provisional regional examples may remain clearly labelled and do not establish automatic synonym equivalence.
- Shared-library improvements should follow observed usage and genuine gaps. No arbitrary merchant-count thresholds or fixed review cycles are set.
- The conceptual allergen, dietary and certification safeguards in this document are approved for F02.
- This approval does not authorise a database schema, application implementation, architecture or jurisdiction-specific regulatory policy.
- No further classification research is required as a prerequisite for continuing F02.

## Source basis and limitations

The starter list is a synthesis rather than adoption of one consultant’s taxonomy. Relevant inputs include: Consultant 1 D1 §§A–D and D2; Consultant 2 D1 §§1–3, D2 sample and embedded D4 examples A–B; Consultant 3 D1 §§4.A–D and D2 Classification Catalogue, Course & Role, Meal Occasions, Cuisines and Structure Concepts sheets; Consultant 4 D2 and D4; Consultant 5 D1 §§B–D and integrated D2/D4; and Consultant 6 D1 §§5–7, D2 Classification Catalogue/NOTE-01 and D4. The six evaluation outputs document the comparative limits and source conflicts.

Consultant 3’s structured D3 is missing; Consultant 2’s claimed full catalogue is not submitted; Consultant 1 and Consultant 5 have malformed catalogue lines; Consultant 6 has inconsistent hierarchy and synonym records; and several source claims across submissions lack independently inspectable evidence. These limitations do not prevent a modest business-usable starting set, but they limit confidence in detailed regional aliases and exhaustive coverage.

No business needs to wait for future shared-library improvements to create and use its own menu categories or collections.
