# PEWA-F02-R01-C — Business Extensions and Shared-Library Governance

**Status:** Founder-approved product direction for PEWA-F02-R01-C v0.1. Descriptive boundaries only; this does not specify or authorise a schema, API, workflow, architecture or implementation.

## Business menu control

Businesses need not wait for PEWA to add a shared classification before organizing or publishing a menu. A business can:

- Create menu categories and collections that reflect its customers and operations.
- Choose its own customer-facing names, languages, ordering, nesting and presentation.
- Place an item in different business categories or collections on different menus.
- Create a business-specific classification for a local dish, seasonal offer, service point or concept that has no shared PEWA equivalent.
- Use an approved shared reference classification, a business extension, both, or neither.

The business-facing category remains the business’s presentation choice. A shared classification is optional guidance that can make related items easier to discover across businesses. It does not require the business to rename, restructure or display its menu in PEWA’s terms.

## Optional mappings

Where useful, a business may associate its own category with one or more PEWA shared classifications for discovery. The business label remains intact. A mapping can be broad, partial, provisional or absent when the meaning does not fit. PEWA should not imply a custom category is an exact match merely because a nearby shared label exists.

An unmapped business category must remain usable within that business’s menu. It can still be reached through the business’s own menu structure and wording. Search or directory behaviour for unmapped terms is a separate discovery question; the lack of a shared match must not block menu publication.

## Shared library and growth

Keep the shared library modest at first. Treat new terms and classifications reported by businesses as **candidates**, not automatic shared entries and not errors in the business menu.

When actual use shows a recurring need, a future shared-library review can consider:

1. Whether the new term represents a food/beverage identity, a preparation, a menu presentation, a role, an occasion, a modifier or an attribute.
2. Whether existing entries already cover the need without erasing local meaning.
3. The available evidence, regional scope, confidence and known ambiguity.
4. Whether a new shared entry, a scoped example, a synonym, or no shared change best serves discovery.

No merchant-count threshold, automatic promotion, fixed review cycle or mandatory business remapping is proposed. Shared governance concerns changes to the optional common library; it does not govern what a business may call or display on its own menus.

## Change without breaking business menus

Shared-library additions and refinements should be additive where practical. A new shared entry should not silently rename or reorganize business categories. If a shared label is later revised, existing business display names and menu placements should remain as the business set them. Any future migration or suggested mapping should be explicit, reviewable and non-destructive, with the reason and previous label traceable.

If a shared entry is later split, combined or retired, businesses must not be forced into an automatic one-to-one mapping where the prior meaning no longer fits. Preserve the old association as history or an unresolved match until the business chooses otherwise. These are intended product boundaries; exact storage and migration mechanisms are outside this document.

## Presentation versus shared governance

| Business control | Shared-library governance |
|---|---|
| Create and use local categories, collections and labels | Decide whether a term should be offered as an optional PEWA-wide classification |
| Choose whether or how to map a local category | Review evidence and semantic scope for a possible shared entry |
| Change display name, section order and menu grouping | Keep the shared library understandable and avoid unsupported synonym merges |
| Publish a category without a PEWA match | Record candidate terms and observed gaps for later consideration |

No central approval is needed merely to use a category in that business’s own menu. Shared inclusion and business use are different decisions.

## Candidate record guidance

If PEWA later tracks candidate additions, retain the term as supplied, business context, optional proposed shared match, region/language when supplied, confidence and the observed gap. Do not turn an unreviewed merchant label into a verified cultural synonym, ingredient, allergen state or certification. This is guidance for future review, not a data schema.
